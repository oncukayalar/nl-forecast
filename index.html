"Days" is scored days / days with a forecast but missing actuals. The last four columns are benchmarks, each built with what was known when the model's forecast was issued. "Yesterday" is the same hour of the latest fully known day, "Last week" the same hour a week earlier. "Day-ahead price as forecast" (imbalance targets) is the error of the day-ahead price of the same hour, taken as the forecast of the imbalance price; it uses only day-ahead prices that were published when the imbalance forecast was issued. "Day-ahead price + usual spread" adds to that price the median, over the last 28 days known at the issue, of the imbalance price minus the day-ahead price in the same hour of the day: the feed price lies below the day-ahead price on most hours and the take price a little above it on average, so the price alone is a weak forecast, most of all of the feed price, and this is the benchmark an imbalance forecast has to beat. rMAE is the model MAE divided by the last-week MAE, the relative error used in the electricity price forecasting literature; below 1 is better than that naive forecast. The last-week forecast is a weak benchmark in this market, so the table also gives the model MAE divided by the lowest benchmark MAE of the row. Method

Twice a day a job runs: at 11:30 Europe/Amsterdam it issues the day-ahead price forecast for the next day, before the auction closes at 12:00; at 15:30 it issues the imbalance price forecasts for the next day. Each run fetches data from the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform (day-ahead prices, load forecast, wind and solar forecasts, imbalance prices) and the morning's ECMWF weather forecast for ten points in the Netherlands and Germany (wind speed at 100 m, solar radiation, temperature), issues what is due (the day-ahead forecast with TimeH2O-v1 (H2O.ai, checkpoint timeh2o-v1), a pretrained time-series model used without further training, with custom features built from the price history, the Dutch holiday calendar and the weather forecast for the target day; the imbalance forecasts with the mean of three models per target: LightGBM, a gradient-boosted tree model trained every day, and two pretrained time-series models used without further training, Chronos-2 (Amazon) and TimeH2O-v1 (H2O.ai, checkpoint timeh2o-v1)), fetches the actuals that have been published since, and rescores. When the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform has no Dutch day-ahead price for a day whose auction has cleared, the job takes it from EnergyZero, whose hourly prices agreed with ENTSO-E's to within 0.01 EUR/MWh from November 2024 to November 2026. All series are the hourly mean of the published values; since the day-ahead market moved to 15-minute products the day-ahead target is the hourly mean of the four 15-minute prices, stitched onto the hourly history before the switch. The imbalance targets are hourly means of TenneT's imbalance prices, read from the ENTSO-E Transparency Platform, which has one price per 15 minutes: the take price is what a party pays for a shortage, the feed price what it gets for a surplus. Both markets settle per quarter-hour; an hourly mean leaves out the shape within the hour, which matters most for the imbalance prices. The day-ahead forecast is issued at 11:30 the day before delivery since 2026-11-20; before that it was issued at 15:30 two days before delivery. Imbalance forecasts are issued one day ahead. When the ENTSO-E data for the next day is missing at 15:30, the job tries again every hour until 19:30 and then issues with the data there is; the issue time of every forecast is in its file. A forecast is never edited after it is issued: a missed run leaves a gap, nothing is backfilled.

On a day TimeH2O-v1 cannot make the day-ahead forecast, Chronos-2 or LightGBM makes it; the model label of the forecast shows which. The imbalance forecasts are this blend since 2026-11-18: the three models have equal weights, a model that cannot run on a day is left out, and the model label of each forecast lists the models that made it.
